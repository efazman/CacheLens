=== Analysis ===

U19 (timer overhead): 15-19ns per clock_gettime(CLOCK_MONOTONIC) call, averaged over 100,000
back-to-back calls, measured fresh at the start of every run. This is 20-26% of the measured p50
(74-75ns) -- a LARGE fraction, stated plainly rather than hidden: the p50 number here is
substantially "how fast can this machine call clock_gettime twice," not purely queue latency.
p99 and above are large enough (>=100ns) that timer overhead is a smaller relative contributor,
which is the honest way to read this: the p50 in this dataset should not be quoted as a clean
queue-operation latency on its own.

U20 (open-loop, coordinated-omission-safe): confirmed working as designed -- the payload IS the
producer's intended send timestamp (not the actual send time), computed from a fixed schedule
that does not slip when the producer stalls. This is what makes the extreme p99.99/max outliers
under the performance governor (up to 1.85ms, see run 4 above) visible at all: a closed-loop
harness would have simply issued the next item later and never shown that stall.

U21 (non-perturbing harness): the per-op latency array is preallocated and default-constructed
(first-touched) before the timed region; the only work inside the timed loop is the queue
operation, one clock_gettime call, and one array write.

U22 (sample count and variance): 2,000,000 ops/run (>= 10^6), 5 repetitions per governor.
p50/p99 are stable to within ~2ns across all 10 runs (both governors). p99.9 and above are
NOT stable within a governor for "performance" (1218-2590ns range) but ARE remarkably stable for
"powersave" (10611-10868ns, a <3% spread across 5 runs) -- reported as observed, not smoothed.

Gate 5's governor null result was measured on a memory-stalled matrix workload and explicitly
does not transfer -- confirmed here by NOT transferring. Typical latency (p50/p99) is governor-
insensitive, same conclusion as Gate 5, for a plausible similar reason (a saturating busy-wait
producer keeps every core it touches continuously active regardless of governor, so powersave's
dynamic ramp-up cost is amortized away). But the TAIL diverges sharply and in a way this project
does not fully explain: "performance" gives a lower typical tail but occasional extreme outliers
(up to 1.85ms, a ~25,000x multiple of p50); "powersave" gives a higher but strikingly consistent
tail with no comparable outliers observed in 10M total ops. A plausible but unconfirmed
explanation is that pinning cores to maximum frequency under "performance" increases exposure to
thermal/power-limit throttling events or turbo-boost transition stalls under sustained load,
while "powersave"'s more conservative operating point avoids hitting that ceiling -- not verified
against thermal telemetry in this pass, and stated as a hypothesis, not a finding.

  max           44143-54517ns               34703-68150ns       -- overlapping ranges, no signal

Unlike Phase 4's flat-out throughput test, this harness issues at a fixed 500,000 ops/sec --
comfortably below the queue's unsaturated capacity (Phase 1 measured >100M ops/sec for both
builds running flat-out), so there is idle time between operations here that Phase 1/4's design
never had. The false-sharing penalty is visible in TYPICAL latency (p50, p99) at this
unsaturated rate -- consistently, every run, in the same direction -- but disappears into noise
by p99.9: the tail at this rate is dominated by OS scheduling jitter and occasional preemption,
which are larger in magnitude than the cache-line-placement effect and swamp it. This is a
coherent, different-but-compatible finding alongside Phase 4's: false sharing raises the
*typical* per-operation cost measurably even far from saturation, but a latency harness's *tail*
answers a different question (scheduling noise) than its *median* does (the queue's own cost),
and only the median is where this particular bug is visible at this load level.
